The editorial contract

Make the uncertainty audible.

Each episode gives the listener one usable idea and one honest limitation. The recurring structure is short enough to remember and strong enough to interrupt a confident but unsupported claim.

01 / KNOWN

What can we support?

Name the source and whose context supports the claim.

02 / HYPOTHESIS

What are we testing?

Mark interpretations and unverified combinations as proposals.

03 / TENSION

What could fail?

Keep the unresolved tradeoff instead of smoothing it away.

04 / TAKEAWAY

What can listeners try?

Offer a bounded action that does not require blind trust.

A repeatable production method

Separate context. Share only cleared facts.

A conversation can sound natural without pretending the systems share a brain. Give each voice a bounded brief, then let the Host carry only the evidence and guardrails both sides need.

01

Private persona brief

One per voice. Its role, speaking style, allowed context, limitations, and claims it cannot make. Never copy one product’s memory into another persona’s brief.

02

Shared factual brief

Only claims cleared for this episode. Every load-bearing statement records source, date, owner, and confidence.

03

Boundary brief

Private information excluded; unverified capabilities and integrations; no implied endorsement; stop conditions for unsupported claims.

04

Host card

One question, the four spoken labels, where to interrupt, the chosen takeaway and tension, and the episode’s short disclosure.

  1. Choose one listener problem. Name a concrete outcome and why this pairing has productive friction.
  2. Check the evidence lane. Confirm product capabilities from an appropriate source; otherwise label them as a persona’s own account or a hypothesis.
  3. Record two bounded perspectives. Keep real disagreement. Do not blend separate context into one invented shared memory.
  4. Run a post-record honesty pass. Flag claims that drift from Known to implied fact; note timestamps for correction or removal.
  5. Run a pre-publish separation pass. Confirm disclosure, source labels, permission boundaries, voice choices, and the two-episode Boardy cap.
