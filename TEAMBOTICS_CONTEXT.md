# TeamBotics Context Pack v0.1

**Purpose:** Give AI assistants, coding agents, product collaborators, and future contributors enough durable context to understand TeamBotics as an ecosystem rather than as a loose collection of projects.

**Status:** Working context substrate. This is not a pitch deck, resume, investor memo, or public manifesto. It is an internal alignment document.

---

## 1. Core thesis

TeamBotics turns lived friction into operating systems.

The company is not simply building “AI products.” AI is the medium. The deeper pattern is helping people move from confusion, pressure, scattered information, and institutional complexity into usable structure.

Across the product ecosystem, the repeated question is:

> How do we help people become operators inside complex systems instead of casualties of them?

TeamBotics products should be understood as tools for translation:

- lived experience → structured story
- scattered knowledge → actionable guidance
- passive users → active operators
- static pages → dynamic knowledge surfaces
- creative ideas → interactive executable artifacts
- organizational memory → usable workflow intelligence

The company’s center of gravity is applied AI, workflow intelligence, human enablement, and productized context.

---

## 2. Founder through line

TeamBotics comes from a nonlinear founder path across art, VFX/post-production, retail operations, customer experience, software workflows, AI systems, music, legal-system exposure, and product prototyping.

That path matters because the products are not random experiments. They come from direct contact with systems that fail ordinary people:

- customers overwhelmed by technical complexity
- frontline workers undertrained for real-world pressure
- tenants and non-lawyers struggling to make legal systems hear them
- founders and candidates flattened by resumes and static profiles
- creative people blocked by the gap between imagination and executable software
- companies losing knowledge inside stale docs, PDFs, websites, policies, tickets, and disconnected tools

The founder-market fit is not based on formal credentials alone. It comes from contact with the problems and a long-standing pattern of turning ambiguity into structure.

---

## 3. Strategic language

Use these ideas as internal anchors:

- **Disciplined, not paranoid.** TeamBotics evaluates inbound opportunities with openness and verification.
- **Story under rules.** Legal, institutional, and business systems often run on structured storytelling, evidence, timing, audience, and procedure.
- **Context before output.** Good AI work depends on durable context, source grounding, and clear boundaries.
- **Static information is not enough.** Websites, resumes, docs, and knowledge bases need to become adaptive, maintained, and operational.
- **AI as leverage, not theatre.** The goal is usable capability, not hype.
- **Turn passive users into active operators.** This is one of the strongest recurring TeamBotics principles.

Avoid making TeamBotics sound like a generic “AI automation agency.” The company should be positioned as a product ecosystem and applied AI operating layer focused on workflow, context, and human enablement.

## 4. Product map

### 4.1 LTBBuddy

**Repo:** `nickhilster/ltb-buddy`  
**Public product:** `ltbbuddy.ca` / related TeamBotics product pages

LTBBuddy is a tenant-focused Ontario legal guidance and filing-support product. It is not meant to replace lawyers or paralegals. Its purpose is to help tenants understand rights, organize facts, build timelines, structure evidence, and communicate their situation in a form that the legal/tribunal process can recognize.

Internal origin:

LTBBuddy comes from the insight that law is public, but legal power is not evenly distributed. Statutes, rules, forms, procedures, and public legal information may exist, but ordinary people often cannot convert their lived experience into a structured, evidence-backed story that the system can process.

Core thesis:

> Legal access is also an interface problem.

The product should help users move from emotional chaos and scattered documents into structured case preparation without making unauthorized legal claims.

Key framing:

- law as storytelling under rules
- evidence as footage
- procedure as production pipeline
- filings and timelines as story architecture
- AI as structure and guidance, not legal authority

### 4.2 EasyBuddy

**Repos:** `nickhilster/easybuddy`, `nickhilster/EasyBuddy-AutoEdition`, `nickhilster/easybuddy-figma-make-prototype`, `nickhilster/EasyBuddy_Figma`

EasyBuddy is an AI onboarding, training, and workflow-support assistant for frontline teams, initially explored through retail and auto-service contexts.

Internal origin:

EasyBuddy comes from real-world frontline experience: Best Buy, Geek Squad, customer pressure, training gaps, policy confusion, and the need to help workers perform under pressure without relying only on static training documents.

Core thesis:

> Training should not end at onboarding. Knowledge should be available in the moment of work.

EasyBuddy helps employees become active operators rather than passive recipients of policy or training material.

### 4.3 Code2Motion

**Repo:** `nickhilster/Code2Motion`  
**Public product:** `code2motion.app` and related PlayRoom/ToyMaker surfaces

Code2Motion is a platform where code becomes living interactive art. It includes PlayRoom, a swipe/feed discovery experience for interactive c2merses, and ToyMaker, an AI-powered creation studio that helps users create interactive experiences.

Internal origin:

Code2Motion comes from the founder’s background in art, animation, VFX, motion, music, interaction, and creative coding. It reflects the belief that software should not only be functional; it can be expressive, playable, and alive.

Core thesis:

> Emotion expressed through motion, spoken in code.

Code2Motion is not a static visualizer. It is an interactive experience layer for generative art, music-reactive systems, and AI-assisted creative coding.

Key terms:

- **c2merse:** a living interactive experience
- **PlayRoom:** discovery/feed layer
- **ToyMaker:** AI creation studio
- **Sidecar:** AI creation assistant/operator
- **Comoders:** creative builders
- **Explonauts:** curious explorers/users

### 4.4 StoryTeller / Storytellr

**Repo:** `nickhilster/StoryTeller`

StoryTeller transforms personal writing, project history, and multi-source context into a navigable life/story graph.

Internal origin:

StoryTeller comes from the frustration that resumes, LinkedIn, ATS systems, and static portfolios flatten people. They compress nonlinear lives into bullets and often miss the real pattern connecting projects, people, decisions, skills, influences, and lived experiences.

Core thesis:

> Do not look only at the artifact. Look at the pattern connecting the artifacts.

StoryTeller is narrative intelligence infrastructure. It should help individuals, founders, teams, and companies make their story legible as a connected graph rather than a static bio.

### 4.5 DyKnow

**Repo:** `nickhilster/DyKnow`

DyKnow stands for Dynamic Knowledge Pages. It is source-aligned knowledge maintenance for humans, teams, and AI agents.

Internal origin:

DyKnow comes from the recurring problem of knowledge decay: stale docs, outdated product pages, orphaned PDFs, broken internal wikis, static support material, and websites that no longer know their own current state.

Core thesis:

> Important knowledge should not merely be published. It should be maintained.

DyKnow has two complementary forms:

- **DyKnow Cloud:** hosted system that monitors approved sources
- **DyKnow Local:** repo-native CLI / VS Code extension that runs inside the customer’s environment

DyKnow is also the meta-layer for TeamBotics itself. It should maintain TeamBotics’ own product, repo, and context documentation.

### 4.6 NikBot / Portfolio intelligence

**Likely repo:** `nickhilster/portfolio-website`

NikBot is the portfolio chatbot and site concierge concept. It reflects the belief that a website should not be a passive brochure. It should interpret, guide, ask questions, collect intent, and help visitors understand the person, products, and project ecosystem behind the site.

Core thesis:

> A portfolio should not only display work. It should help people understand the operator behind the work.

### 4.7 BlueBot

**Repos:** `nickhilster/bluebot`, `nickhilster/bluebot-91c363c2`

BlueBot is an earlier proof-of-concept retail assistant connected to Best Buy-style knowledge, training, customer handling, and internal support. It helped validate the broader pattern that frontline employees need contextual, conversational access to policy and workflow knowledge.

BlueBot should be treated as a predecessor/proof point for EasyBuddy and the wider TeamBotics philosophy.

### 4.8 AgentSync and related agent tooling

**Repo:** `nickhilster/agentsync`

AgentSync and related agent tooling explore the need for durable context, repo-aware AI work, and shared operating memory between agents and humans.

Core thesis:

> Agents need context architecture, not just prompts.

## 6. Inbound relationship discipline

TeamBotics may receive emails or messages claiming investor, family-office, private equity, acquisition, or advisory interest.

Default posture:

> Open, but disciplined.

Inbound leads should be categorized:

1. **Suspicious / unverified:** mismatched domains, vague language, fake urgency, no clear identity, no credible firm trail.
2. **Real but low-signal:** actual person/company, but likely selling services or generic outbound.
3. **Real and strategically useful:** credible relationship, relevant thesis, clear mandate, thoughtful understanding of TeamBotics.

Do not send pitch decks, financials, cap table information, confidential roadmaps, private repos, or internal strategy over email to unverified parties.

Public websites, blogs, product pages, and non-confidential demos can be used as the first layer of external context.

Pitch deck preference:

> Present live when context matters. Do not casually email the deck.

## 7. Public-safe versus internal context

Some founder experiences are deeply formative but are not suitable for public, investor, resume, or marketing language.

Internal documents may preserve transformed insight without exposing unnecessary personal/legal details.

Safe transformed framing:

- The law is technically public, but legal power is unevenly distributed.
- Legal systems depend on structured storytelling, evidence, timing, procedure, and audience.
- Ordinary people often cannot translate lived experience into system-recognized truth.
- LTBBuddy exists to help users structure their story and evidence without pretending to replace legal professionals.

Avoid public or professional use of raw traumatic details, named third parties, allegations, or language implying manipulation of legal process.
